How to use it

  1. Who it is from and to. Your name and how to reach you, then the client.
  2. Number and dates. Number invoices in order (INV-001, INV-002…) and give a due date; 15 or 30 days is common.
  3. Items. One line per service or period: hours or quantity, and your rate.
  4. Tax, if you charge it, with its label and rate.
  5. How to pay you. Bank, GCash, Wise or PayPal details, so the client never has to ask.
  6. Download PDF and choose Save as PDF in the print dialog.

An invoice made here is a request for payment. If you are registered with the BIR, ask your accountant which of your invoices need to be BIR-registered.

Questions

Is this invoice generator free?

Yes. Fill in the form, then choose Download PDF and save it from the print dialog. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit.

Is my invoice uploaded anywhere?

No. The invoice is built in your browser and never sent to a server. A draft is kept in this browser so a refresh does not lose it; Start over clears it.

What should an invoice include?

Your name and contact details, the client's name, an invoice number, the issue and due dates, each item with quantity and rate, the total in the currency you are billing in, and how to pay you. More on what goes on an invoice.

Which currencies can I invoice in?

Philippine peso, US, Canadian, Australian and Singapore dollar, British pound, euro and Japanese yen. USD and CAD invoices use US Letter paper; the rest use A4, the same as the Singil app.

Is there an app that remembers my clients and rates?

Singil for iPhone saves each client's rate and billing schedule, picks the next billing period for you, and makes the PDF offline. It costs ₱99 once ($1.99 in the US), with no subscription.
