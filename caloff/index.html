How it works

Point the camera. That's the whole flow.

  • Scan

    A photo of the plate goes straight to a quantised vision model on the device. The picture never leaves the phone, so there is nothing to upload and nothing to wait for on bad signal.

  • Correct

    The model's guess is a starting point, not a verdict. Rename the dish and the calories follow the name through a local database of 1,065 foods — Filipino home cooking, the chains, and the Western shelf — so the correction sticks and the food is one search away next time.

  • A whole tray

    A wider shot is read as what it is: rice, a viand and a drink come back as three rows with their own portions, not one row named after whichever dish the camera saw first.

  • Re-log

    Yesterday's lunch goes back in the log at one tap, at the portion you already reviewed. No inference, no second guess.

  • On the wrist

    An Apple Watch and Wear OS app that shows the numbers and nothing else, plus home-screen widgets, a Lock Screen complication and a Wear tile.

Deliberately absent

No feed, no badges, no cloud fallback.

There is no social feed, fasting timer, sleep tracker or workout log. There is no account. There are no ads, no data sale, and no free tier funded by either — CalOff is paid for by the people using it, which is why it can afford to want nothing else from them. And there is no "just this once" server call when a scan is hard — the moment that exists, the one thing that makes CalOff different is gone.

Privacy Policy

Effective 3 September 2026

CalOff does not collect your data, because it has nowhere to send it. There is no CalOff account, no CalOff server and no CalOff analytics. This policy explains what that means precisely, including the one time the app uses the network.

What stays on your device

All of it. Specifically:

  • Meal photos. A photo you take or pick is analysed by a model running on the phone and is not uploaded anywhere. CalOff does not copy it into its own storage; it is read, analysed and released.
  • Your food log — dish names, portions, calories, macros and timestamps — is written to a database inside the app's private storage on the device.
  • Your profile — age, sex, height, weight, activity level, goal and unit preference — which is used only to compute your calorie and macro targets, on the device.
  • Your weight history, if you record weigh-ins.
  • Foods you save to the local food database.

None of this is transmitted to us or to anyone else. We cannot read it, and we could not produce it if we were asked to.

The one network request

Before the first scan, CalOff downloads the vision model it runs — a file of roughly 728 MB — from Hugging Face, a public model host. It is a plain file download, started once and resumable.

That request necessarily reveals to Hugging Face what any file download reveals: your IP address, the time of the request and which file you asked for. CalOff attaches no identifier, no account and no information about you to it. Hugging Face's handling of that request is governed by their privacy policy. After the download completes, the app makes no further network requests — scanning, nutrition lookup and search are entirely local, and CalOff works with the device in airplane mode.

What we do not do

  • No analytics or telemetry SDK of any kind.
  • No crash or performance reporting service.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, no third-party trackers.
  • No sale or sharing of personal information — there is none to sell.
  • No cross-app or cross-site tracking, so CalOff never needs to ask for tracking permission.

Permissions, and why

  • Camera — to photograph a meal. The live preview runs inside the app; frames are used for the scan and nothing else.
  • Photo library — only when you choose to log a meal you already photographed, and only for the image you pick.
  • Internet (Android) — for the one model download described above.

You can refuse any of these. Refusing the camera or photo library means you log meals by search or by hand; the rest of the app is unaffected.

The watch and widget companions

The Apple Watch and Wear OS apps, the home-screen widgets and the Lock Screen complication display the same day's totals that the phone app already holds. Those figures are passed from the phone to the watch over the platform's own local pairing channel — Apple's Watch Connectivity or Google's Wearable Data Layer — which is a direct link between your own two devices. They travel no further, and no server of ours is involved.

Children

CalOff is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. Since the app collects nothing at all, there is nothing for us to delete on request — but see the next section for how to remove everything from the device.

Deleting your data

Two ways, both immediate and both complete:

  • Settings → Reset inside the app clears the food log, profile and weight history.
  • Delete the app. iOS and Android remove the app's private storage with it. Nothing survives elsewhere, because nothing was ever elsewhere.

Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the CCPA/CPRA and the Philippine Data Privacy Act give you rights of access, correction, deletion, portability and objection over personal data a company holds about you. We hold none: CalOff processes your information only on your own device, under your control. You exercise every one of those rights directly, by opening, editing or deleting the data in the app. If you would like this confirmed in writing for a compliance review, write to us and we will confirm it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the revised version is posted on this page with a new effective date. A change that would materially reduce the protection described here would be announced in the app's release notes as well.

When something has gone wrong, the three things that make it fixable are: your device model, the OS version, and what you were doing at the moment it happened. A screenshot beats a description.

The first scan is stuck downloading

The model is a single 728 MB download. If it stalls, it resumes from where it stopped rather than starting again — reopen Settings → Scanner and start it once more. On a metered connection, wait for Wi-Fi: this is the only download CalOff will ever ask you for.

The calories look wrong

They probably are, and correcting them is part of the flow rather than a workaround. Tap the dish name on the result screen and type what it actually was; the nutrition follows the corrected name through the local food database. Adjust the portion in grams if the size was misjudged. What you save is what gets logged, and the food is searchable afterwards.

Scanning is slow, or the app is warm

Inference is real work being done on your phone rather than in a data centre, so it costs battery and takes a few seconds. There is one model and every phone runs the same one, so the answer does not change with the hardware — only how long it takes. If it still struggles, log by search: it is instant, and it is the same database.

My watch shows nothing, or yesterday's numbers

The watch app mirrors the phone; it never talks to a server. Open CalOff on the phone once with both devices nearby and unlocked, and the watch and tile catch up. Check that CalOff is installed on the watch, and on Android that the phone and watch are paired in the Wear OS app.

I switched phones and my log is gone

The log lives only on the device, which is what keeps it private and also means there is no cloud copy to restore. A full device backup — iCloud device backup, or Google's backup on Android — carries it across; a fresh install does not. We cannot recover a log that was not backed up.

How do I delete everything?

Settings → Reset clears the log, profile and weight history immediately. Deleting the app removes everything else with it. See the Privacy Policy for what "everything" covers.

Refunds

Purchases are handled by the stores, so refunds go through them: reportaproblem.apple.com for the App Store, or Google Play's order history on Android. We cannot issue one directly, but tell us what went wrong — that is how it gets fixed for the next person.
