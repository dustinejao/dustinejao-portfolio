BMR and TDEE are not the same

BMR is what you burn lying still: breathing, circulation, keeping warm. TDEE adds digestion, walking to the jeep, climbing stairs and any training. Most people burn 20–70% more than their BMR in a day, which is what the activity factor captures.

Getting the activity level right

  • Sedentary (1.2): desk job, commute by car or ride, little walking.
  • Light (1.375): on your feet part of the day, or 1–3 workouts a week.
  • Moderate (1.55): 3–5 real workouts a week.
  • Active (1.725): hard training 6–7 days, or physical work all day.

When in doubt, pick the lower one. Overestimating activity is the most common reason a deficit does not work.

Questions

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure: the calories your body uses in a day, including resting metabolism, digestion, daily movement and exercise. Eating at your TDEE keeps your weight steady.

How do I calculate my TDEE?

Work out your BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiply by an activity factor: 1.2 if you sit most of the day, 1.375 for exercise 1–3 days a week, 1.55 for 3–5 days, 1.725 for hard exercise 6–7 days.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

It is an estimate from averages, usually within a few hundred calories. The truest number is in your own log: if your weight holds steady over two or three weeks of honest tracking, what you ate on average is your TDEE.

How many calories below TDEE should I eat to lose weight?

About 500 a day is the common starting point, roughly 0.45 kg (1 lb) a week at first. Keep women above 1,200 and men above 1,500 kcal unless a health professional says otherwise.
