How it is worked out

  1. SSS: your salary is matched to a monthly salary credit in ₱500 steps from ₱5,000 to ₱35,000; you pay 5% of it.
  2. PhilHealth: 5% of salary, split evenly with your employer, so 2.5% for you, on salary between ₱10,000 and ₱100,000.
  3. Pag-IBIG: 2% of salary (1% at ₱1,500 or less), capped at ₱200.
  4. Withholding tax: on salary minus those three, using the BIR's monthly table in force since 2023.

Questions

How much is the take-home pay of a ₱25,000 salary?

About ₱22,611.20 a month in 2026: ₱1,250 SSS, ₱625 PhilHealth and ₱200 Pag-IBIG come off first, then ₱313.80 withholding tax on the rest.

How much is the take-home pay of a ₱30,000 salary?

About ₱26,542.45 a month, after ₱2,450 in contributions and ₱1,007.55 tax.

How much is the take-home pay of a ₱50,000 salary?

About ₱42,231.60 a month, after ₱3,200 in contributions and ₱4,568.40 tax.

What are the SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG rates in 2026?

SSS: 5% of your monthly salary credit for the employee (₱5,000 to ₱35,000, so at most ₱1,750). PhilHealth: 2.5% for the employee on salary between ₱10,000 and ₱100,000 (at most ₱2,500). Pag-IBIG: 2% up to ₱200. The employer pays its own share on top.

How much salary is tax-free in the Philippines?

Under the TRAIN law tax table in force since 2023, the first ₱250,000 of taxable income a year is tax-free: about ₱20,833 a month after contributions.
