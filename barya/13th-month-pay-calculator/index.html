How it is worked out

  • Basic salary earned = monthly basic salary × months worked − unpaid absences.
  • 13th month pay = basic salary earned ÷ 12.
  • Taxable part = whatever 13th month pay and other bonuses add up to above ₱90,000.

If your salary changed during the year, add up each month's basic pay instead of multiplying, and divide that total by 12.

Who gets it

Under Presidential Decree No. 851, rank-and-file employees in the private sector who worked at least one month in the calendar year are entitled to it, whatever the position, how they are paid, or whether they have since resigned. It is prorated for anyone who did not work the full year.

Questions

How is 13th month pay computed?

Add up the basic salary you earned from January to December and divide by 12. On a ₱25,000 monthly basic salary for all twelve months, that is ₱300,000 ÷ 12 = ₱25,000. Overtime, allowances and most other pay are not included, only basic salary.

When is 13th month pay given?

On or before 24 December, under Presidential Decree No. 851. Employers may pay half earlier, for example in June, and the rest by 24 December.

Is 13th month pay taxable?

Only above ₱90,000. Under the TRAIN law, 13th month pay and other bonuses are tax-free up to ₱90,000 a year combined; anything above that is added to taxable income. Bills to raise the ceiling were filed in 2026, so check the latest BIR rules before you file.

Do I get 13th month pay if I resigned or started mid-year?

Yes, prorated. Rank-and-file employees in the private sector who worked at least a month in the year get one-twelfth of the basic salary they actually earned, whether they resigned, were let go or joined late.

Are unpaid absences deducted?

Yes. 13th month pay is based on basic salary actually earned, so unpaid leave and absences lower it. Paid leave counts as salary earned.
