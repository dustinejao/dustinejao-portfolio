How the numbers are worked out

  • 50/30/20: your take-home pay times 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2.
  • Daily budget: (take-home pay − fixed bills − savings) ÷ 30. That covers food, transport, load and the small things that never feel like spending.
  • Weekly budget: the daily figure times 7.

The split is a guide, not a law. If your bills already take more than half your pay, the calculator tells you, and the useful move is to trim wants before savings.

Budgeting in the Philippines

  • Start from take-home pay, after withholding tax and SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions, not your gross salary.
  • Count the small daily spends. Jeepney and MRT fares, load, coffee and merienda add up faster than bills; a daily budget is what catches them.
  • Pay yourself first. Move the savings amount on payday, before spending starts, so the daily budget only covers what is left.
  • Use the 13th month pay to build an emergency fund rather than raise everyday spending.

This is a general guide to budgeting, not financial advice for your situation.

Questions

What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?

Split your take-home pay three ways: 50% for needs (rent, food, transport, bills), 30% for wants (eating out, shopping, subscriptions) and 20% for savings and paying down debt. It was popularised by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in their 2005 book All Your Worth.

How do I budget a ₱25,000 salary?

With 50/30/20, a ₱25,000 take-home pay is ₱12,500 for needs, ₱7,500 for wants and ₱5,000 for savings. Once fixed bills and savings are set aside, what is left over the month works out to a daily spending budget; the calculator above does that division for you.

How do I calculate a daily budget?

Take your monthly take-home pay, subtract fixed bills and the amount you are saving, and divide what is left by the number of days in the month. That is what you can spend each day on everything else: food, transport, load, small purchases.

Does 50/30/20 work in the Philippines?

As a starting point, yes, but needs often take more than half in Metro Manila once rent and transport are in. If they do, shrink wants before savings. Even 10% saved is a habit you can raise later, and 13th month pay is a good time to top up an emergency fund.
