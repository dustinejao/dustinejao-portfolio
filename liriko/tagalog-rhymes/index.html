How the finder decides what rhymes

Tagalog spelling follows the sound closely, so the finder reads syllables straight off the word: pu·so, a·la·a·la, pa·nga·rap.

  • Two-syllable rhymes share the last two syllables from the first vowel on: pangarap and sarap, alaala and akala.
  • Last-syllable rhymes share the final vowel and anything after it: buhay, kamay, tunay.
  • Same root, different affix is not a rhyme. Buhay and mabuhay are one word wearing two outfits, so the finder leaves them out.
  • Common words first. Words from a hand-kept Tagalog lexicon come before words that only appear in a large corpus of everyday Filipino.

The word list comes from Liriko's Filipino rhyme dictionary: words attested in a corpus of real Filipino usage (FrequencyWords, MIT licence) plus a lexicon kept by hand, each one checked to parse as Tagalog syllables. Slurs and explicit words are left out of suggestions.

Questions

How do I find Tagalog words that rhyme?
Type the word into the finder above. It splits the word into syllables and lists every word in Liriko's Filipino dictionary that shares its last two syllables (the strongest rhymes) or its last syllable (the classic tugma), common words first.
What words rhyme with puso?
Two-syllable rhymes: uso, suso, mauuso. Last-syllable rhymes ending in -o: ako, dito, ano, aso, dugo, buo, gusto, mundo, tayo and many more. See all rhymes for puso.
What is tugma?
Tugma is rhyme in Filipino poetry and song: lines that end in matching sounds. Traditional Filipino verse pairs it with sukat, a fixed syllable count per line.
What is a two-syllable rhyme?
A rhyme that matches across the last two syllables, like alaala and akala, instead of only the last one. Rappers call it a multi. It lands harder and sounds more deliberate.
Is there an app that finds Tagalog rhymes offline?
Yes. Liriko, for iPhone, iPad and Android, shows Tagalog and English rhymes while you write, from dictionaries stored on the phone, so it works with no signal. It is free to start.
