How to write rap in Tagalog

Paano sumulat ng rap sa Tagalog

By Dustine Jao, maker of Liriko. Updated 27 November 2026.

Write to the beat, know the punchline before the setup, end every line on a rhyme and stack two-syllable rhymes inside it, keep each bar’s syllable count steady, and lean on words that mean two things.

Seven steps

  1. Pick the beat first. Write to the tempo you will rap on. Tap the BPM, loop the beat, and write while it plays so every line already fits the pocket.
  2. Write the punchline, then the setup. Know the line you are building to before you write the lines that lead there. The setup exists to make the punchline land.
  3. End lines on a rhyme, then rhyme inside them. Start with end rhymes, then add internal rhymes in the middle of the line. Two-syllable multis are what make a bar sound written.
  4. Count syllables per bar. A bar is four beats. Lines that carry roughly the same number of syllables keep the flow steady; change the count on purpose to switch the flow.
  5. Use words with two meanings. Tagalog is full of them. A word that means two things at once is a ready-made punchline.
  6. Switch languages where it hits. Drop an English word where the rhyme or the rhythm needs it, not at random.
  7. Rap it out loud, then cut. Record a take. Any syllable you trip over twice is one too many.

Rhyme: end, internal, multi

An end rhyme closes the line. An internal rhyme lands inside it. A multi matches two or more syllables. Stack all three and a single line does a lot of work:

Sa harap ng pangarap, tinikman ko ang sarap

Harap, pangarap and sarap all end in "-arap": three two-syllable rhymes in 14 syllables. Find more in the rhymes for pangarap.

Tagalog makes multis easier than English in one way, because words are spelled as they sound: if the last two syllables are written the same, they rhyme. Find them with the Tagalog rhyme finder.

Double meanings

Some of the most useful words in Tagalog mean two different things. Put one where both meanings work and the line pays off twice:

WordMeaning 1Meaning 2
mahallove, dearexpensive
bukastomorrowopen
arawsunday
buwanmoonmonth
ligawlost, straycourting

Mahal kita, pero mahal din ang bigas

The second mahal flips the first: love, then expensive. The listener hears the turn a beat after you say it, which is what a punchline is.

Flow: syllables per bar

A bar is four beats. How many syllables you fit into it is your flow: around eight to twelve sits comfortably at a mid tempo, more makes it a double-time run. Count the syllables of each line; Liriko shows the count on the line you are writing. Keep lines that share a flow within a syllable or two of each other, and break the pattern only when you mean to.

Practice that works

  • Rhyme ladders. Pick a word, list every two-syllable rhyme for it, and write one bar per rhyme.
  • Timed freestyles. One minute on one topic over a loop, recorded. Keep the lines that surprised you.
  • Rewrite a weak bar three ways. Same meaning, different rhyme each time.

Questions

How do you write a rap in Tagalog?
Loop the beat and write while it plays. Know your punchline before the setup, end lines on rhymes and add internal two-syllable rhymes, keep each bar's syllable count steady, use words with double meanings, and record a take to cut what trips you up.
What is a multi in rap?
A rhyme that matches across two or more syllables, not just the last one: pangarap, sarap and harap share "-arap". Multis are what make a verse sound crafted rather than easy.
How many bars are in a verse?
Sixteen is the most common verse length in hip-hop, with an eight-bar hook. Battle rounds and freestyles run longer and looser.
