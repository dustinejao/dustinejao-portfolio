Beats for the
Filipino people.

Tunog is a marketplace for Philippine instrumentals. Producers upload a beat and set what a licence costs; artists listen through the whole catalogue, buy the one they want, and pay in pesos with the wallet already on their phone. No foreign card, no dollar conversion, no waiting.

Live and taking uploads
The thing you actually use

The player never stops.

A beat keeps playing while you search, open a producer's profile, read a licence or fill the cart. The bar at the bottom of every page is one audio element that outlives the route, so nothing restarts underneath you and no tab ends up fighting another for the speakers.

Plays only count once a listener has stayed past a minimum, is rate limited per session, and never count when the producer is the one listening — so the number on a beat is worth something.

Payments

Pay with what you have.

GCash, Maya, GrabPay, ShopeePay or a card, settled in pesos through PayMongo. The thing that stops most Filipino artists buying a beat is the checkout, so that is the part that got built first.

Uploading

A beat, not a form.

Drop the audio, add cover art, set BPM, key, genre and your prices. The file goes straight to object storage from the browser, so a full-length WAV does not have to squeeze through a server first.

Discovery

Search that hears you out.

Look by title, producer, genre, key or tempo, and keep the results playing as you go. Favourite a beat, follow a producer, and the homepage starts to look like what you actually make.

Producers

You set the price.

Every licence tier is yours to name, per beat. Your profile is a real page at tunog.net with your own handle, your catalogue, and plays, likes and downloads you can read without guessing.

For producers

Free to start, and it stays free.

Free

Three uploads a month, plays, likes and downloads on a dashboard, and a 15% cut on a sale. Enough to find out whether anyone wants what you make.

Pro — ₱499/mo

Unlimited uploads, buyer insights and traffic sources, a shot at the homepage feature, discount codes and promos, and the cut drops to 5%.

Your page

tunog.net/yourhandle, with your avatar, header, bio and catalogue. It is a link you can put in a bio, not a profile buried inside someone else's app.

Your files

Audio, stems and cover art live in object storage and are served straight from the edge, so a listener half a country away gets the same first second you do.

How it got built

Built with Jerick Nucasa.

Tunog is a two-person build. Jerick set the thing up and carries the backend; my end of it has been most of what a listener touches — search and discovery, favourites and following, the cart and checkout screens, the mobile layout, the file handling that puts uploads in object storage, and the deploys.

It runs on Next.js with the App Router, Convex for the data and its live queries, Clerk for accounts, Cloudflare R2 for the audio and artwork, PayMongo for the peso payments, and Vercel underneath the lot.
